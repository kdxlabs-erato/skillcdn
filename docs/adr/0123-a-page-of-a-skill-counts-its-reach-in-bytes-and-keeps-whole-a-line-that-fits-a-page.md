# ADR-0123: A page of a skill counts its reach in bytes, keeps whole a line that fits a page, and says how many pages are left

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-22
- Amends point 1 of [ADR-0113](0113-a-page-of-a-skill-ends-after-a-line-and-a-later-part-does-not-say-the-skills-facts-again.md) (the line a page ends after, and the word), points 1 and 2 of [ADR-0118](0118-a-page-of-a-skill-ends-between-two-blocks-and-says-what-it-left-out.md) and point 1 of [ADR-0122](0122-a-text-that-begins-after-other-text-on-a-page-is-cut-within-the-whole-reach.md) (what the reach is counted in). The rest of them stays.

## Context

A skill's context is read in pages of 16,384 bytes, and a page ends where a reader can stop: between two blocks of Markdown within 4,096 characters of where the bytes run out, else after a line within 2,048, else between two words within 256 ([ADR-0118](0118-a-page-of-a-skill-ends-between-two-blocks-and-says-what-it-left-out.md), [ADR-0113](0113-a-page-of-a-skill-ends-after-a-line-and-a-later-part-does-not-say-the-skills-facts-again.md)). Three things were wrong with that, and a fourth was found by measuring.

**A line could be cut in two.** A row of a table, a line of code or a paragraph written as one line, longer than 2,048 characters, was cut between two words when a page's bytes ran out in it. The 36 skills of two served packs read 114 files of 8,351 lines: ten lines are longer than 2,048 characters, and three of them, two paragraphs of some 4,500 characters and a row of 2,092, would be cut where a page end fell in their last part. None is cut today, by where the page ends happen to fall. A line cut in two is a row with half its cells or a link with half its address, and nothing said on the next page mends it, as a table's head said again mends a table cut after a row. The pages of a file already end after their last whole line, however far back ([the tools](../specs/tools.md#raw-files)).

**The reach was counted in characters, and the page in bytes.** Where every character takes three bytes, as in Hangul, a page holds a third as many characters and the same reach was half of it: a page of such text could end half full, where a page of English is three quarters full at least. The guide had to give an author two numbers.

**An author could not see where the pages of a skill end.** A page end moves with everything above it: a sentence added to the rules of a pack moves every page end of every skill under them. `skillcdn check` said in how many pages a skill is read, and not where they end.

**A reader told only that there is more stops reading.** A page that was not the last said so at its head, with the call that reads the next, 16 KiB above where the page ends. Two command-line agents were given a task that names a skill of four pages or of seven, served from a directory by this command (2026-11-22). Codex 0.171 with its default model, and Claude Code 2.1.330 with its largest model, read every page, six runs of six. Claude Code with Sonnet 5.5 and with Haiku 5.5 read the skill of seven pages to its end in none of six runs: they stopped after two to five pages and answered from what they had. The skill of four pages they read to its end in three runs of four.

The same runs measured what a client does with a result that is long, since that bounds a page ([the tools](../specs/tools.md#results-and-continuations)). Codex cuts the output of a tool over some 48,000 bytes, a resource read among them: it read the skill of four pages as the document the skills extension serves, 61 KB in one piece, and its model was given the head and the tail with 14 KB cut from the middle. It then read the pages, in each of the four runs where it had read the document first.

## Decision

1. **The reach is counted in bytes.** A page ends between two blocks within 4,096 bytes of where its bytes run out, a quarter of a page, and for the text a page begins with no further back than half of the bytes the page could hold of it. A text that begins after other text, with no such place in the 4,096 bytes or fewer the page could hold of it, begins the next page. A line longer than a page is cut between two words within 256 bytes.
2. **A line that fits a page is not cut.** With no place between two blocks in reach, a page ends after its last line end, however far back, when the line its bytes run out in is one a page holds whole. A text that begins after other text, whose first line does not fit what is left of the page, begins the next page.
3. **A line longer than a page is cut between two characters as a reader sees them.** With no space in reach, the page ends where its bytes run out, and not before a character that is read as part of the one before it: a combining mark, a joiner or a selector, the vowel or the final consonant of a Hangul syllable written in its letters, the tone of skin of an emoji. A page looks back over 32 characters for such a place, and a longer run is cut as it is.
4. **A page says how many pages are left, at its head and at its end.** A page of a skill read in more than one counts the pages after it, as they will be cut. The tools say it in the line that names the next call, and once more as the last lines of the page, with the same call and that the skill is not to be used or answered from yet. The last page says that the skill is complete. The REST API answers the count as `pagesLeft`. The command, whose parts are runs of pages and which ends a part with its own line, says as before.
5. **`skillcdn check` says where a page of a skill ends inside something.** Under each skill, a line for every page that ends inside a table, a code block or a line, or right after a heading or a line that opens what follows, with the file and the line the next page begins at. A note, `page_cut`, counts the pages that end inside a code block or a line. It fails nothing.

## Consequences

- **Text of one byte a character is paged as before.** The 36 skills read again take the same 113 pages, and every page ends where it did.
- **Text of three bytes a character has fuller pages and a shorter block kept whole.** A page is three quarters full at least, where it could be half full, and a block is kept on one page up to 4,096 bytes, some 1,300 characters, where it was some 2,700. The same skills with every word replaced by Hangul take 160 pages under both rules; the shortest page is 12,445 bytes where it was 10,608, and four pages end inside a table, after a row, where two did.
- **A page may end early before a long line.** A line of 12 KiB that begins 2 KiB into a page leaves that page at 2 KiB. Lines longer than the 4,096 bytes a page gives up for a block are few, and each costs one page at most.
- **With the count and the call at the end of a page, the smaller models read on.** The same runs again: Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 read the seven pages to the end in ten runs of eleven, and the four pages in four of four; the run that stopped did so at the third page, where the skill names a tool the session did not have. A page is some 300 bytes longer for it.
- **Cursors handed out before the change stay good**, since they are offsets into the same context, and the reading rules do not change: pages are cut when they are read.
- **`@skillcdn/core` gains** `contextPageCuts`, `CONTEXT_BLOCK_REACH` and `ContextCut`; `countContextPages` takes where to count from, and `contextLinesPage` what a page holds. `SkillResult` and the REST skill gain `pagesLeft`.
- **Rejected:**
  - a reach of half a page for every script, which keeps whole a block of up to 8 KiB: the served skills take the same pages, and a skill of blocks between a quarter and a half of a page would take up to twice as many, where the number of pages is what a reader has to get through;
  - a larger page, 32 KiB for one: half the pages, and little room under what the clients measured cut, some 48,000 bytes in one and 40,000 characters in another, with clients not measured whose bound is not known. A page that is cut by a client loses text without a word, which a reader that stops early does not;
  - telling a page by number, `page 3 of 7`: a page the response budget cut short moves the count of the pages before it, and the count of those after it is what a reader acts on;
  - the boundaries of characters from the runtime's own segmenter: the command and a deployment run different versions of it, and `check` would count pages a deployment does not serve;
  - saying the lines before a cut again on the next page: the pages joined would no longer be the text.
