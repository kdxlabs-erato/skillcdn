# ADR-0124: What reaches an agent is bounded in bytes on either route, and a part says what a page says

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-22
- Amends point 1 of [ADR-0108](0108-the-command-prints-what-a-host-shows-whole-and-a-long-skill-in-parts.md) (what the bound of a read is counted in) and the line that ends a part in its point 2, and the last sentence of point 4 of [ADR-0123](0123-a-page-of-a-skill-counts-its-reach-in-bytes-and-keeps-whole-a-line-that-fits-a-page.md) (the command's parts say no count). Builds on [ADR-0110](0110-the-reads-of-the-command-are-a-library-for-other-commands.md) (the reads of the command are a library). The rest of them stays.

## Context

An agent reads a pack by one of two routes: the tools of an MCP connection, or the `skillcdn` command run in a shell. Each answer reaches its model through a host that bounds what it gives the model of one answer, and past the bound the host cuts without the agent being able to ask for what was cut. The two routes were bounded in different units and said different things at the end of a piece, and a program built on the command had to guess.

**The command counted characters, and the hosts count bytes.** ADR-0108 bounded a read for an agent at 28,000 characters. Two hosts were run with outputs of known length (2026-11-22). Claude Code 2.1.330 showed its model a command's output whole at 29,500 bytes, and saved one of 30,300 bytes to a file, showing the first two kilobytes; an output of 60,000 bytes of Hangul, 21,500 characters, was saved too, so the bound is in bytes. Codex 0.171 showed 38,000 bytes whole, and of 43,000 bytes the head and the tail, with the middle replaced by a line that says how many tokens were cut: 10,000 tokens counted at four bytes each. A skill written in Hangul, 39,745 characters and 93,449 bytes, was printed in two parts of 49,851 and 44,468 bytes, and a page of a file in it of 56,769 bytes. Each passed both hosts: in one it went to a file, in the other its middle was lost. The MCP route is bounded in bytes already (ADR-0034, ADR-0123).

**Only the skill was bounded.** The listing of `skillcdn skills` was printed whole: 36,833 bytes for the reference pack, 66,167 for the reference console's, 52,844 for this repository's. A page of a folder or of a search was bounded by its number of entries alone.

**A part said less than a page.** ADR-0123 had a page say how many pages follow it, at its head and again at its end with the call that reads the next, and the last page say that the skill is complete: with that, two smaller models read a skill of seven pages to its end in ten runs of eleven, where they had in none of six. A part the command prints was left as it was, ending `The skill continues. Read the next part before using it:` and the command, and the last part ended with the skill's text. The reference console prints skills through `readPack` (ADR-0110) and found on that route what the measurement found on the other: a fresh agent read one part of four and planned from it. So the console added sentences of its own after the library's line: that the part stops in the middle and the command is to be run now, how many pages follow, kept from the count of each cursor it had seen, and that the last part is the last. To know whether a part went on it looked at whether the text ended with a line it had made, since `readPack` answered text alone and asked for the line of every run of pages it tried, printed or not.

**What a page shows of a skill was the MCP route's alone.** The bounds on the lists of a skill's first page (its files, its references, its metadata, its warnings) and the facts a later page leaves out were in the MCP replies; the command had its own, shorter version of the second and none of the first.

## Decision

1. **Every bound on what reaches an agent is counted in bytes, and is `@skillcdn/core`'s.** `MCP_RESULT_MAX_BYTES` bounds the serialized tool result (24 KiB, as before) and `COMMAND_OUTPUT_MAX_BYTES` what one read of the command prints for an agent: 28,000 bytes, under both hosts measured. Every read of the command keeps within it. A skill is printed in parts, each a run of the same pages a client reads over MCP. A page of a file is the length asked of the pack (`FILE_PAGE_CHARS`, 24,000 characters, unless `--limit` says otherwise) where it prints within the bound, else as much of it as does, ended after its last whole line, and it says where the next begins. A page of a folder, of an outline or of a search that would not print within the bound is asked for again with fewer entries, so that the pack names the cursor that goes on. The listing is printed in pages where the caller says how to read on (the command's `skillcdn skills --cursor`), each ending with that command; a caller that says nothing is given it whole, as before, since what a page left out would be lost for certain to a reader that cannot go on. `OUTPUT_CHARS` stays exported, deprecated, as the same number.
2. **A part says what a page says.** A part that is not the last says how many pages of the skill follow it, at its head with the command that prints the next part, and again at its end: that what follows is part of the instructions, not to use the skill, plan from it or answer from it yet, and to read the next part now and each one after it until a part says the skill is complete; its last line is the command, alone. The count is of pages, as the pack counts them (`pagesLeft`, ADR-0123), so both routes say the same number, and it never says less than what is left. Against a pack that counts no pages, a deployment before 0.6.1, a part says the rest without the count. The last part of a skill read in parts says `Skill context is complete: this was its last part.` A skill printed whole says nothing of parts.
3. **One shaping and one text for both routes.** `skillShown` and `fileShown` of `@skillcdn/core` are what one answer shows of a page of a skill and of a file: the text whole, the lists bounded in bytes and what was shortened named, and a page after the first without the skill's facts. The MCP replies and the command both use them. `renderSkillResult` takes, beside the names of the reads, the line that reads on (`ToolWords.loadNext`), and words the closing sentences in the caller's word for one read (`piece`). A program with commands of its own hands in its words and its line and prints the same sentences as the command; it adds none of its own.
4. **`readPack` answers where a read goes on.** Beside its text it answers `next`: the cursor of the next part of a skill with the pages still to read, the cursor of the next page of a folder, an outline, a search or the listing, or the offset a file continues at; nothing where the read reached the end. A program acts on these and reads no text for them. `nextPart` only makes the line: it is called for every run of pages a read tries, printed or not.

## Consequences

- **The smaller models read a skill to its last part.** Two command-line agents were given a task that names a skill of the reference pack, of four pages or of seven, read with the command in their shell, once with 0.6.1 and once with this change (2026-11-22, Claude Code 2.1.330 with Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5, Codex 0.171 with its default model, each without a person). With 0.6.1, Sonnet read to the last part in none of five runs, stopping after two parts of four or three of seven, and Haiku in two of five; with the change both did in every run, ten of ten. Codex read every part in all six runs with either. A task that asks for a code written in the middle of the skill in Hangul was read to its end in every run with the change, six parts with nothing saved or cut; with 0.6.1 Claude Code saved four of the parts its model was given to a file and Haiku read them back from there, and Codex was given parts of 49,217 and 45,251 bytes, over its bound, and named the code in both runs all the same, which its events do not show it was shown.
- **A part of a skill in Hangul fits both hosts.** The skill of 93,449 bytes is printed in six parts of 12,644 to 17,590 bytes, where it was two of some 45,000 to 49,000. A skill in English is printed in as many parts as before: a later part some 360 bytes longer for what its end says, and a first part shorter where its lists are long (25,362 bytes of the reference pack's skill of seven pages became 20,435).
- **A part is one page in practice.** Two pages of 16 KiB with a part's head pass 28,000 bytes, so a part joins two only where the second is short. The parts and the pages of a skill are counted alike.
- **The listing of a pack is printed in pages.** The reference pack's in two, of 27,939 and 12,713 bytes, and the reference console's in three: the first with what a client is told on connect and the counts of the pack, a later one saying that it goes on from the page before, and the last that it is the last.
- **The command's first part shows what a first page shows over MCP:** the supporting files up to 3 KiB of paths, five references resolved, the description, metadata and warnings shortened where they are long, with a line that names what was shortened.
- **The reference console can drop the sentences it adds to a part, the count it keeps per cursor and the test of how a part ends**, and with `next` know where a read goes on. A console whose listing of a long pack should not be cut gives `nextPart` for the listing and takes `--cursor` for it.
- **`@skillcdn/core` gains** `COMMAND_OUTPUT_MAX_BYTES`, `fitsCommandOutput`, `skillShown`, `fileShown`, `utf8Length` and `ToolWords.loadNext`; `@skillcdn/cli` gains `ReadOutput.next` and a cursor for the `skills` read. What is printed changes as above; the reading rules, the pages, the cursors and the MCP text do not.
- **Hosts are known whose bounds are below a page.** Read in their source (2026-11-22), not run: Cline cuts a tool result over 8,000 characters in its middle, VS Code's agent writes one over 8,192 characters to a file and gives its model the path, Zed keeps the first 16 KiB of a command's output, Cursor shows 20,000 characters of one, and Roo Code shows ten kilobytes of one and keeps the rest in a file. A page of a skill over MCP is up to 24 KiB and a part up to 28,000 bytes, so each passes them. A smaller page is a change of the reading rules for every pack, and more pages are more chances for a reader to stop; it is to be decided on runs with those hosts.
- **Rejected:**
  - `readPack` answering the state of a part and the console choosing its own sentences, without the command saying them: two texts for one read again, which ADR-0110 exists to end;
  - counting parts rather than pages: how many parts are left depends on the bound and on what a part's head takes, while the pages are the pack's count and the same on both routes;
  - paging the listing for every caller: a reader with no command for the next page would lose the rest for certain, where a host loses only what passes its bound;
  - a setting for the bound: no host measured needs less than 28,000 bytes, and a host that does cuts the page of a skill as well, which a setting of the command does not change.
